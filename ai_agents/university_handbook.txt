Section 1: Attendance Policies and Condonation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Standard Attendance Requirement:
Every enrolled undergraduate and graduate student must maintain an aggregate 
attendance of at least 75% across all registered lecture, practical, and seminar 
sessions each semester to be eligible for end-of-semester final examinations.

1.2 Chronic Absenteeism & Debarment Warning:
Any student whose attendance falls below 70% at the mid-semester evaluation mark 
is automatically categorized as "High Academic Risk" and served an official Debarment 
Warning. Continued failure to improve attendance above 70% before final examinations 
results in formal debarment from taking course exams in the affected subjects.

1.3 Medical Exemption & Condonation Process:
Students with valid medical grounds, bereavement, or authorized university representation 
may apply for attendance condonation for absences down to 65%. Applications must be 
submitted within 7 business days of the absence to the Dean of Academic Affairs, 
accompanied by verified institutional medical certificates. Absences below 65% cannot 
be condoned under any circumstances and mandate course repeating.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 2: Continuous Assessment & Remedial Class Directives
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) Standards:
Students are evaluated continuously through quizzes, laboratory assignments, and 
internal midterm assessments scaled out of 100 marks. The minimum acceptable passing 
threshold for internal continuous assessments is 45 out of 100.

2.2 Mandatory Remedial Classes for Struggling Students:
Any student scoring below 45 marks in their internal evaluations is required to attend 
Mandatory Remedial Tutoring sessions. These sessions are held twice weekly by course 
faculty and certified peer tutors. Attendance in remedial sessions is tracked directly 
and accounts for up to 10 compensatory recovery marks on the student's final internal score.

2.3 LMS Portal Engagement Guidelines:
Students are required to access the university Learning Management System (LMS) 
regularly to review lecture notes, submit weekly problem sets, and participate in discussion 
boards. A login frequency of fewer than 15 logins per evaluation cycle is flagged as 
academic disengagement and prompts mandatory outreach by course coordinators.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 3: Merit & Need-Based Financial Aid Waivers
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Scholarship Retention Benchmarks:
Recipients of university merit scholarships and fee waivers must maintain a minimum 
internal continuous evaluation score of 60 out of 100 and an attendance rate of 80% 
or higher in every academic term to retain funding.

3.2 Emergency Scholarship Grace Period & Appeals:
If a scholarship recipient encounters sudden academic distress (internal score falling 
between 45 and 59, or attendance dropping between 70% and 79%), they are placed on 
Financial Aid Observation for a 30-day grace period. During this window, the student must 
meet with an academic advisor and sign an Academic Recovery Commitment Agreement. 
Financial aid is not revoked immediately; rather, funding is sustained through the 
probationary term provided satisfactory progress is documented.

3.3 Low-Income Hardship Assistance:
Students experiencing severe financial hardships impacting their course materials or 
campus commute may apply for an emergency textbook voucher and transportation subsidy 
through the Student Financial Aid Office.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 4: Academic Probation and Early Intervention Protocol
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Triggers for Academic Probation:
A student is formally placed on Academic Probation under any of the following conditions:
(a) Internal assessment marks fall below 45 in two or more enrolled subjects.
(b) Composite AI Academic Risk Score exceeds 0.70 (High Risk status).
(c) Chronic attendance deficit below 60% without medical condonation.

4.2 Terms of Academic Probation:
Students placed on Academic Probation are subject to the following mandatory stipulations:
1. Registration for subsequent terms is capped at a maximum of 15 credit hours (restricted load).
2. Mandatory bi-weekly check-in meetings with the designated departmental Academic Advisor.
3. Suspension from holding elective student government offices or intercollegiate athletic participation.
4. Completion of a customized 3-Step Academic Recovery Plan agreed upon by the student, 
   advisor, and department chairperson.

4.3 Removal from Probation:
Probation status is lifted at the conclusion of the subsequent term if the student achieves 
an attendance of >= 75%, passes all internal evaluations with scores >= 50, and fulfills 
all remedial attendance requirements.
